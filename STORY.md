# The Story

I dispatch dump trucks for a living.

Nineteen drivers. Stone quarries. Asphalt plants. Concrete pours. Every morning at 4 a.m. I already know which driver missed his alarm, which plant is backed up, and which job site is going to call me by 6:30 asking where the hell their loads are. I know because I've been doing this long enough that it's in my bones.

I am not a software engineer. I never went to college for computers. I didn't grow up around technology. I grew up around work.

But I have five sons — ages ranging from grade school to adulthood — and I watch them stepping into a world that is getting reshaped by AI faster than anyone can explain. I watch Big Tech build tools for people who already have everything. I watch cloud companies charge monthly forever for things a decent computer used to own outright. I watch privacy get sold as a feature instead of a right.

And I decided I wasn't going to accept that for my family.

So I bought a Raspberry Pi 5 for about $100. I bought two NVMe drives and put them in RAID so I wouldn't lose anything. I installed Ollama — a free, open-source tool — and ran a real AI language model on it, locally, on my desk, not in anyone's cloud. I built my own dispatch intelligence system. I filed a patent on a portable AI device that runs completely offline. I built a mental health platform with real crisis integration. I did all of it while dispatching 19 trucks by day and putting kids to bed at night.

November 16, 2025 is the date I write down when people ask me when it clicked. That was the day I got everything working on a USB stick in about two hours — after a full day of previous attempts had failed because I was fighting complexity instead of using simplicity. I wrote it down because I knew I'd need to remember it. That's what this repository is: me writing things down so I don't forget them.

This is also a book.

Not someday. Now. It's being written right here, one chapter at a time, in public, version-controlled, no gatekeepers. It's for people like me — people who work real jobs and manage real responsibilities and thought AI was something other people got to have. People who are about to lose Windows 10 security support and don't know what comes next. People who are tired of paying cloud subscriptions for tools that could just live on a computer they already own.

The book is called *The Pragmatic Systems Builder: One Dispatcher's Guide to Owning Your Own AI.*

You can follow along as I write it. Start with [Chapter 1](./book/ch01-why.md).
